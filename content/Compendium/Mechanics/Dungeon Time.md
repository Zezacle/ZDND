[[Dungeon Time]] describes the gameplay of scenarios where the party is acting in a situation that is neither combat, roleplay, travel, resting or downtime. Dungeon Time can be used to explore dungeons, buildings, attend a party to spy on an sketchy noble--any scenario where it makes sense to arbitrate player turns in 10-minute increments known as [[Stretch|Stretches]].

Players can enter Combat Time while in a dungeon and then resume Dungeon Time (often starting a new [[Stretch]]) when combat closes.

### Stretches and Activities

While in Dungeon Time, a player can perform an [[Activity]] during each [[Stretch]] such as, *Keep Watch*, *Search Area*, *Loot Everything* and more. [[Activity|Activities]] usually have an associated ability check and may have a prerequisite to perform. There is a list of predefined [[Activity|Activities]], however they are a malleable concept and the DM can improvise a ruling on a unique [[Activity]] a player wishes to perform--which is to say feel free to think outside the box.

#### Penalties of Taking Too Long
Players should be aware that things happen in the world around their character out of sight. Every [[Stretch]] spent looting or checking for traps is time that advances, which could mean a monster wandering through the dungeon finds them or some other event happens.

### Entering Dungeon Time

When players enter a scenario that would qualify for Dungeon Time, it starts when the DM decides, when the first [[Activity]] is declared, or when the first 10 minute duration starts (via spell or torch being lit). 

#### Marching Order
When entering Dungeon Time, the DM may ask for a **Marching Order**, for scenarios where the party is exploring potentially dangerous areas. A marching order is who is in the front of the group, who is in the back and the order of the rest of the party in between. The marching order can be freely changed, but must be explicitly declared. (eg. "My character is going first through this corridor." or "My character was a distracted when we leave this room, I'm now at the back of the group.")

Depending the size of the space being traversed, the party may be forced into a *Single File* marching order for 5-foot-wide corridors or a *Double File* marching order for most interior spaces at least 10-feet-wide.

#### Default Initiative
For Dungeon Time scenarios with many minor combat encounters, the DM can request a **Default Initiative** from the players to avoid repeatedly rolling [[Initiative]]. The players decide who goes first in combat and then if the group wishes to go clockwise or counterclockwise around the table.

**Shuffling the Default.** While not in combat [[Initiative]], the party can choose to change who goes first in the Default Initiative.

> [!info] Online Tables (Discord)
> When playing online, the direction of the initiative can be alphabetically or the reverse as most voice/video apps sort participants of a call alphabetically.

#### Visibility
When using Dungeon Time to explore a dark area such as a *dungeon,* it is important determine how the party is handling visibility.
- **Determine Light Sources.** The party should declare what light sources they have available and who is bearing them. For consumable sources, such as [[Torch|Torches]] or [[Liquid Light]]. If these sources do not have a cost (eg. [[Emit Light]] cantrip) it is assumed the player maintains a 100% uptime unless explicitly interrupted by something like the [[Incapacitated]] condition.
- **Darkvision and Special Senses.** Characters with [[Darkvision]] or other [[Special Senses]], should remind the DM of the type and range they have.

> [!Warning] "I have Darkvision!!!"
> In [[Darkness]], characters with [[Darkvision]] make [[Perception]] checks at [[Disadvantage]], which decreases [[Passive Perception]] by -5.

#### Passive Perception
The DM might ask players for their [[Passive Perception]] if they don't already have it saved somewhere. Your [[Passive Perception]] will determine your ability to detect hostiles, traps (if you aren't actively searching for them), or notice inconspicuous details of your surroundings such as the Kobold tunnels dug into the wall at ground level.
